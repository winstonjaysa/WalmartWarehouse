I designed and built a live 3D warehouse inventory dashboard. 🏭 It started with a client inquiry: a fittings manufacturer wanted to move their warehouse off paper and Excel. Instead of replying with only a quotation, I built a working concept so they could see exactly how their warehouse would run. What I built: 🏗️ An interactive 3D model of the warehouse in Three.js, with racks, bins, receiving, packing and dispatch zones, a moving forklift and docked trucks 📦 Clickable rack bins: tap any bin to see the item, code, batch/lot, quantity, reorder level and movement history 🔍 Search that flies the camera straight to any item or bin 🟢 A stock-level view that colours every bin by status, with warning pins on low-stock locations 📊 Live KPIs, an activity feed and order tracking from picking to dispatch 📱 A mobile app prototype for warehouse staff: receive goods, put away, pick and stock count All of it is driven by barcode scanning. Every carton and rack bin gets a QR/barcode label. Staff use a Bluetooth barcode scanner or their phone camera: scan the carton, scan the bin, done. No typing, no paperwork. The parts I enjoyed most were under the hood: → Data design: stock is never a number someone types over. Every scan writes a movement record, and balances are calculated from those movements, so every figure can be traced back to who scanned what, and when. → Integration: I planned the sync with Accounting software / POS system, so accounting and the warehouse floor always show the same numbers without double entry. → Thinking like the user: I designed the flows around how stock actually moves on a warehouse floor, not around what's easy to build. Stack: Laravel, Filament, Vue/Ionic for the mobile app, Three.js for the 3D dashboard, and barcode/QR scanning. I build custom web systems for businesses that have outgrown spreadsheets: inventory, operations dashboards, internal tools and integrations. If you need a system like this, or you're hiring someone who can take an idea from client conversation to working product, let's talk. 👋 #Laravel #ThreeJS #WebDevelopment #FullStackDeveloper #InventoryManagement #WarehouseManagement #BarcodeScanning #Malaysia #OpenToWork

    My client thanked me for something most developers never do — telling the truth.  A client approached me for a consultation on their e-commerce system. One of the first decisions was hosting: shared server or dedicated.  I gave them both options honestly:  Shared Server (through a partner):  - Cheaper — saves about RM 300-400 per year  - But the data sits on someone else's infrastructure  - If anything happens to that provider, your business goes with it  - Payment processing issues on their end could affect your store  - You share traffic, resources, and risk with others  Dedicated Server (your own):  - Costs more upfront  - But the data is 100% yours — you hold the keys  - Payments go through your own account — full financial control  - No shared traffic, no dependency on a third party  - You decide everything  I laid out the pros and cons clearly. No sugarcoating, no pushing towards the option that benefits me more.  His response surprised me:  "Thank you for sharing this with me, Afif. Not many people are willing to be this transparent. Most just stay quiet."  I told him:  "I'd rather my clients understand every option clearly so they can make the right decision. I don't want misunderstandings down the road. You choose what's best for your business — I'll build whatever you decide."  I even shared a referral link for the server so he could get 2 months free — at least covering some of the initial cost.  ---  The lesson:  Transparency isn't a weakness in business — it's a long-term strategy.  When you're honest about trade-offs, clients trust you. When clients trust you, they come back. When they come back, they refer others.  I've never lost a client by telling the truth. But I've seen many lose clients by hiding it.  Be the developer your clients don't have to second-guess.  #ClientRelations #SoftwareEngineering #Transparency #Trust #DeveloperLife #BusinessEthics #ECommerce

     From LMS to Complete Digital Ecosystem 🛒💬💳  Sometimes one project becomes three — and that's exactly the point.  A client's Learning Management System was "complete" — students could enroll, learn courses, earn certificates. But the business needed more.  "Afif, can students also buy our physical products? And maybe a community forum for discussions?"  Challenge accepted. Times three.  The Scope:  What started as "add a shop" quickly revealed its true complexity:  - Complete e-commerce marketplace with categories, products, variants  - Community forum with threaded discussions  - Second payment gateway for transaction flexibility  - All integrated seamlessly into the existing LMS  The Complexity:  Each system alone would be a project. Together, they required careful orchestration:  Marketplace (24 commits):  - Product categories with hierarchy  - Products with multiple images and size/edition variants  - Real-time stock tracking with status calculation  - Shopping cart with localStorage persistence  - Full CRUD for categories, products, images, variants  Forum System (21 commits):  - Categories with staff-only posting option  - Discussion posts with open/closed status  - Threaded comments with unlimited nesting  - Solution marking for answered questions  - Hierarchical close permissions (creator/author/staff)  Payment Gateway (7 commits):  - Bayarcash integration alongside existing ToyyibPay  - Checksum verification for security  - Webhook endpoint for payment callbacks  - Tab-based settings UI for dual gateway management  The Architecture:  Before writing code, I mapped the full-stack flow for each system:  - 5 new database migrations (V48-V52)  - 7 new JPA entities with complex relationships  - 7 repositories with search, filter, pagination  - 8 DTOs for clean API responses  - 4 services with comprehensive business logic  - 4 controllers with public + authenticated + admin paths  - 20+ Vue.js components with consistent UX patterns  The Implementation:  With architecture clear, execution followed the same pattern for each system:  Entity → Repository → DTO → Service → Controller → Frontend  Consistency across all three systems meant faster development and easier maintenance.  The Delivery:  Complete Digital Ecosystem with:  ✅ E-Shop Marketplace (categories, products, variants, cart)  ✅ Community Forum (categories, posts, threaded comments, solutions)  ✅ Dual Payment Gateway (ToyyibPay + Bayarcash)  ✅ Super Admin management dashboards  ✅ Public-facing interfaces  ✅ Seamless LMS integration  Total commits: 55  Total development time: 3 hours.  The lesson? An LMS isn't just about learning content — it's about building a complete ecosystem where students can learn, buy, discuss, and engage. The best systems grow with their users' needs.

    Automation That Haunt Me 👻 I came into automation from web development, where I loved designing things to be reusable. I thought that instinct would protect me. It didn't. User: "We just need this report from this Excel." Me: Simple request, standalone script, done. User: "For this payment method, use this percentage." Me: Hardcode it. New payment method next month? Hardcode that too. Repeat until it's clearly not sustainable. User: (Given an Excel to key in their own rates) "Why is it giving wrong numbers?" Me: Check the file. Same payment method, two different percentages. Same method, no percentage at all. Not carelessness — I'd handed someone a spreadsheet and expected it to behave like a form. What they needed was a UI, probably? I didn't know that until it broke. User: "The dashboard shows this number, why doesn't your report match?" Me: Dig in. Their input was wrong. But to them, it's still my automation that's broken. Now I'm maintaining their data, not just my code. User: "Can you run this every month automatically?" Me: Manually re-running scripts in a terminal, every time — until I find screen -S to keep a session alive on the server. Feels like hiring a quiet assistant ✨ . User: "The automation stopped updating." Me: Check the session — dead. I hadn't touched anything. Server got rebooted, and screen doesn't survive that. No warning. Just silence until someone noticed. User: "Remember the last automation? Use its output for this new one." Me: Now automations feed automations. One broken link upstream, everything downstream is wrong — silently. User never sees any of this. To them, once it's built, it's built. A lifetime tool. What actually changed things for me? I stopped treating every automation as a one-off. Input → process → output — once I saw that shape, I built a reusable template instead of writing scripts from scratch each time. I stopped duct-taping servers and found proper task scheduling — set once, survives a reboot. I stopped copy-pasting the template into every job. Now every task gets its own folder, its own config. New job? Drop a folder, write the job-specific logic, set the trigger. The core stays untouched. Maintenance finally had a place to live, instead of being scattered everywhere. Why this matters, especially now To the user, automation is magic. To the developer, it's a spell that keeps calling you back — invisible from the other side of the dashboard. Also, with AI making everything look instant, users now expect automation to behave the same way — immediate, effortless, no one behind the curtain. But the part that actually takes time was never writing the code. It's designing the right solution before the wrong one comes back to haunt you. I've gone through templates, schedulers, restructured folders — and every one felt like "the fix" until the next problem showed up wearing a different disguise. #Automation #SoftwareDevelopment #DevOps #DeveloperLife #LessonsLearned
